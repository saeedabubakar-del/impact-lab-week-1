IMPACT LAB – WEEK 1 MISSION

Research Brief: How the Clinton Health Access Initiative Uses Data for Impact in Africa

Research Topic: Data-Driven Healthcare Transformation in Africa
Organization: Clinton Health Access Initiative (CHAI)
Country of Focus: Nigeria
Prepared by: Abubakar Saeed

1. Who Are They?

The Clinton Health Access Initiative (CHAI) is a global health organization working with governments and partners to strengthen healthcare systems and improve access to affordable, quality healthcare, particularly in low- and middle-income countries.

Across Africa, CHAI supports governments in addressing public health challenges through innovative solutions, technical expertise, research, and the effective use of data.

One of its important areas of intervention is the digital transformation of public health campaigns, helping health authorities make better decisions and reach more people.

2. What Data Do They Use?

CHAI supports the use of digital data systems to improve the planning, implementation, and monitoring of healthcare campaigns.

The organization works with different categories of data, including:

- Population Data: Information about the number and distribution of people within communities.
- Healthcare Coverage Data: Records showing how many people have received medicines, vaccines, or other health interventions.
- Geographical Data: Information identifying communities and locations where healthcare services are needed.
- Health Campaign Data: Digital records of medicine distribution, vaccination activities, and the performance of community health workers.
- Real-Time Monitoring Data: Information collected and transmitted through digital systems to monitor campaign progress.

These datasets help health officials identify underserved communities, monitor progress, detect gaps, and make timely decisions.

3. What Changed Because of It?

A remarkable example of CHAI's data-driven intervention occurred in Tofa Local Government Area of Kano State, Nigeria, during a 2024 lymphatic filariasis campaign.

Before digitization, health campaign reports were largely paper-based, and information could take approximately 60 days or more to reach the central reporting system.

With digital data collection:

- 92% of campaign records reached the central system within four hours of collection.
- Supervisors identified communities with low coverage during the first two days.
- Seven additional community drug distributors were recruited to improve coverage in Langel Ward.
- Tofa recorded 96% campaign coverage, the highest among the participating local government areas.
- By 2025, the digitization initiative had expanded, increasing the number of people reached from approximately 140,000 to more than 2.5 million.

These improvements demonstrate how timely and reliable data can transform healthcare delivery, strengthen accountability, and help governments make evidence-based decisions.

Conclusion

The work of the Clinton Health Access Initiative demonstrates that data is more than numbers stored in computers; it is a powerful instrument for solving real-world problems.

By transforming traditional paper-based healthcare reporting into digital systems, CHAI and its partners have helped improve the speed of information delivery, identify underserved communities, and strengthen healthcare campaign coverage.

Key Lesson: When data is properly collected, analysed, and applied, it can turn information into action and action into measurable social impact.
